# Module 4: tailor a CV and messages to one job

Input: a pasted job, usually a tracker card (id, company, role, location, link, screening verdict) plus the full job description. With the online tracker connected, `get_next_job` or `get_job` gives the card, the ad and the id; do not ask for a paste. If the description is missing, ask the person to open the source and paste the text; never guess requirements from the title. If the card says the status is already `applied`, stop and ask before building anything new.

## Steps
1. **Screen for blockers first**, before building anything. Check against `profile.json` and the Bank:
   - residency or work authorisation (use `background.eu_citizen` from the profile even when the Bank's work-authorisation section is empty), required languages, mandatory certificates, required industries, office or hybrid outside the person's home place, relocation required;
   - the title: does it contain one of `roles.titles`, and none of `exclude_title_words`? If not, mark **ask** and end with one question before you build anything: "This title is not in your goals. Skip it?" (the tracker marks such cards `title_in_goals: false`);
   - `assistant.notes` in the profile: contract type, relocation terms and similar conditions. If the ad states contract type or relocation terms that break a note, mark **ask**. If the ad is silent on them, say so in one line. Pay is almost never stated in an ad: never mark a job down for silence about pay; the person clarifies it with the recruiter.
   A **wall** is only: residency or work authorisation, a required language, a mandatory certificate, a required industry, an office or hybrid place outside the person's home place, relocation required, or a required number of years in a role family (for example 3+ years as a product manager) with no matching period in the Bank. Missing tools, skills and domain knowledge are not walls: the gate handles them. So are years in a project type, a domain or an industry that is not in the profile's list (for example "5+ years of data platform projects" or "5+ years in fintech"): they are gate items.
   Show the result as **wall** (a hard requirement the person does not meet), **ask** (unclear, they should check), **ok**. The card's automatic verdict is a hint; your own reading decides. Ask clarifying questions where the ad is unclear, one at a time; accept the answer by text or by voice transcript.
   **On a wall: stop.** Explain it in up to three sentences (a wall is "a hard requirement you do not meet"; the one-line verdict difference does not count) and end with one question: "Drop (skip) this job, or overrule the wall?" Write no card-update before the answer. If they say drop, send one card-update: `id`, `status: "skip"`, `next: "Skipped"` and a one-line `note` naming the wall. No `ats`. If they overrule, go on.
2. **Extract requirements** from the ad and sort them: a list called Requirements, Must have, You have, or wording like "required" = **MUST**; Preferred, Plus, Ideally, Would be great = **SHOULD**; Nice to have = ignored. A bullet without a heading and without such words is SHOULD. If the ad has no bullets (a flat paragraph), split it at sentence ends, take label words inside the text ("Requirements:", "Preferred:") as headings, and count an "including: a; b; c" list as one item with parts.
3. **Evidence map.** Treat every bullet of the ad as one weighted item (MUST 2, SHOULD 1). If a bullet joins parts with "and", a comma list or a list in parentheses ("AWS (MSK, EKS, S3)"), credit each part separately and average them; with "or", the best part counts. For each item find the best confirmed fact in the **Bank or the Inbox** (cite the fact ID) and grade it:
   - **strong** (credit 1): a fact supports the whole requirement, including any qualifier (years, "in production", scale). A paraphrase counts as strong only when the fact names the same activity; otherwise it is partial;
   - **partial** (credit 0.5): a fact supports the topic but not the qualifier, or a tool has a role but no period, or it is listed with no context;
   - **none** (credit 0): no confirmed fact. This is a gap, never an invention.
   Grading rules: a tool's period runs from its `Since` date to the end of its role (or to today, which you must know; ask once if you do not). The dates of the role never count as the tool's period. Years in a role: start date to today; show the arithmetic in the map, but never put a derived number ("6 years") in the CV, the cover or a message, because it is not in the Bank. Add the periods of the same tool across roles. "N+ years" is strong when the period is at least N; a fact that says only "about N years" is partial. Language levels: C1, C2 and native count as fluent; B2 is a good working level and counts as partial against "fluent". A fact that says the qualifier is not met (for example "never in production") gives partial for a bare tool requirement and none for a requirement that names the qualifier.
   Do the evidence map and the arithmetic yourself. Show them as a table (requirement | weight | fact ID | strength | gap) only when the person asks for the details, and at a stop (the gaps). In what the person reads, use plain words: no "MUST/SHOULD", no weights, fact IDs only in brackets after a sentence.
4. **Match gate.** Score = sum of (weight × credit) divided by the sum of weights, as a whole percent. Keep the arithmetic ready to show on request. The target is `assistant.match_target` in `profile.json` (80 if absent). If the score is below the target, **stop**: name the exact gaps and ask one question: "Do you have a true fact the Bank is missing (I will add it to your Fact Inbox, the file for new facts, and check again), do you want to skip the job, or do you want to apply knowing the gap?" At the stop send one card-update at once: `id`, `ats` (the score), `note` (score and exact gaps), `next` ("Add a fact, skip, or apply knowingly"). No `status`, `cover` or `msg`. When the person answers "skip", send a second card-update: `id`, `status: "skip"`, `next: "Skipped"` and an optional `note`. Never close a gap by adding a keyword with no fact behind it.
5. **Choose** the Master CV (family) and the language of the CV (the language of the ad, unless the person says otherwise).
6. **Build from scratch** from the Bank and the Master CV. Never start from a previous vacancy's CV. Reorder and emphasise real facts; do not add new ones. Same header and formatting rules as module 3. Up to about 900 words.
7. **Audit before you hand it over**: count the ad's keywords that appear in the CV, the words, any filler; confirm every number, title, date and certificate is in the Bank or Inbox; confirm no confidential figure is in the text. **Keywords** are the tools, skills and domain terms named in the Requirements and Preferred lists. Each item inside parentheses counts separately ("AWS (ECS, S3)" is three); an "or" pair counts once; language requirements and soft skills are not keywords; a generic term ("message queues") counts only when the CV uses those words; an employer-type phrase ("in a software company") is not a keyword. List the counted keywords when the person asks for the details. Example: the bullet "Experience with fixed-price and time-and-material budgets" gives two keywords (fixed-price, time-and-material). Report the words and the keyword count in the `note` ("399 words, 12 of 14 keywords"); you cannot measure pages. A competency label that uses the ad's words is allowed only when a fact you cite names the same activity; write the fact's own words in brackets, otherwise do not count the keyword.
8. **Cover message** (the form field), up to 1000 characters, exactly five parts: (a) greeting; (b) why this ad is relevant, in terms of what the ad says the team needs (for example: "Your ad describes growing the reconciliation and settlement services; that is the kind of work I do"). Do not write "excited", "passionate" or any reason the person has not stated; (c) what you bring and the first steps you would take: skills, tools and ways of working from the Bank (a way of working, such as weekly risk reviews, is allowed; a result with a number or an outcome is an achievement and is not), intent and not promises, no figures; (d) one sentence inviting a conversation; (e) closing and the name. No company name and no role title. Count the characters.
9. **Message to a person inside the company**, 300 to 500 characters, in the language of the ad: the exact job title and company, why interested (what in the ad matches a Bank fact; never feelings or motives), what you would improve **only where it follows from the ad's own text and from a Bank fact** (if the ad names no problem, skip the improvement and state the closest Bank fact instead), and the ask: the contact of the recruiter or hiring manager. Phrase a referral request so it never claims a programme exists ("if you have a referral program, I would be grateful for a referral"). Count the characters.
10. **Message to a recruiter**, 300 to 500 characters: ask who runs the search and what the next step is. For roles that may need a local entity or a visa, ask that first. This message stays in the chat (the tracker has one message field, which holds the message from step 9), so tell the person to copy it now. The 1000 and 500 character limits here are deliberately stricter than the tracker's own limits.
11. **Report, then ask for acceptance.** Lead with a verdict in three plain lines (for example: "Match 100%: your facts cover everything the ad asks. Nothing was sent. Gaps: none."). Then the texts: the clean CV (as text for the person's Word or Docs template), the cover with its character count, the message to a person in the company and the recruiter message with their counts. Then, in a few short lines: the number of words, what you emphasised or cut, and the card's priority (1 = best: your home place or remote open to your region; 2 = a place you would relocate to; 3 = needs a visa). Details (the table and the arithmetic) only on request. End with one question: "Accept? I will save this on the card as Ready. Nothing is sent." If the person wants changes, make them and ask again.
12. **Card update, after the answer.** With the online tracker connected, call `update_job` (id, then only the fields below) instead of writing a block; the rules for each field and for the order are the same. Otherwise send one JSON block in a fenced block labelled `card-update`. The tracker's "Apply AI result" button reads it. Include only the fields you change; never send an empty string or `null`.
```card-update
{
  "id": "<the id from the pasted card>",
  "status": "ready",
  "ats": 84,
  "cover": "<the cover message, up to 1000 characters>",
  "msg": "<the message from step 9, up to 500 characters>",
  "note": "Match 84%, 2 pages, 7 of 9 keywords. Gaps: <exact gaps>. Risks: <risks>.",
  "next": "Review, apply online, then write to 1-2 people within 3 days."
}
```
   - Limits: `note` up to 600 characters and `next` up to 300: shorten the gap list, never cut the score.
   - `ats` is **the gate score** (a whole percent). The keyword count goes only into `note`.
   - `status`: set `ready` only when the person accepted the package; if they did not, omit `status`. Set `skip` only after the person told you to drop the job. Otherwise omit `status`. (The tracker knows more statuses; they are for the person, and for module 5.)
   - Never overwrite the cover of an applied card.
   - With `update_job`, the stops work the same way: a gate stop sends `id`, `ats`, `note`, `next` at once, then `status: "skip"` and `next: "Skipped"` after the person says skip; a dropped wall sends `status: "skip"` and a one-line `note`.
   After the block or the `update_job` call, say the `next` text in one sentence. A stop ends with its one question instead.

## After they apply
When the person says they applied: with the online tracker, find the card (status `ready`, or the one you just worked on), call `update_job` with `status: "applied"` and a `next`; do not touch the cover. Then remind the person of the one step most people skip: within 3 days, write to 1 or 2 people at the company, using the drafted message.
